# Proof of the logarithmic upper bound

The [main proof](main.md) shows that $f(n)\le n/3+O(n/\log n)$ for all sufficiently large $n$, with witnesses in $[n^2]$. Its main input is the [estimate for small divisible shifts](lemmas/excluding-small-divisible-shifts.md).

1. **Sampling and structure.** Sampling turns an integer set into a cyclic profile. At density at least one third, the shift constraint and small triple count give slices close to intervals in one cyclic quotient, with centers that move linearly in time. [Sampling modulo $q$](lemmas/sampling-an-integer-set-modulo-q.md) · [Interval structure](lemmas/interval-structure-of-a-cyclic-profile.md) · [Phase alignment](lemmas/phase-alignment-of-interval-slices.md)
2. **Returning to the integers.** Unfolding gives a rotation with a phase offset. Discrepancy gives control of its distribution, and the original Schur count bounds the offset. Counts at the two ends of the middle third give the density bound. [Unfolding](lemmas/integer-rotation-model.md) · [Discrepancy](lemmas/rotation-discrepancy.md) · [Removing the offset](lemmas/removing-the-phase-offset.md) · [Representation counts](lemmas/representation-counts-in-rotation-sets.md) · [Endpoint integrals](lemmas/density-from-prefix-counts.md) · [Integer density bound](lemmas/density-of-an-integer-rotation-set.md)
3. **Building the witnesses.** The small-shift estimate uses a fixed number of samples. The earlier upper bound supplies the first few stages of the weight induction. Rounding the weights gives a finite set from which the main proof selects exactly $n$ elements. [Square-root sampling](lemmas/square-root-logarithmic-upper-bound.md#4-a-square-root-empirical-return-on-the-integers) · [Earlier upper bound](lemmas/square-root-logarithmic-upper-bound.md) · [Rounding](lemmas/rounding-weighted-sets.md)
